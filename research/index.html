Biblical research

Preliminary reconstructed investigation, November 24, 2026. We approach this from a Bible-first Christian perspective while distinguishing what a text says, what historical evidence supports, and theological conclusions. This is not a transcript of a personal journal. The original November 21 investigation was not recovered.

Chronology and reliability

Isaiah has ancient Hebrew manuscript witnesses: the Great Isaiah Scroll predates Christianity. The Book of Mormon places Alma 4 around 86–83 BCE and Alma 7 around 83 BCE according to its own chronology, but the surviving printer’s manuscript is from 1829–1830. See the Joseph Smith Papers manuscript and publication history. These are different types of evidence. Neither the existence of an ancient manuscript nor a later publication alone proves or disproves divine inspiration. Manuscript variants, authorship and independent corroboration still require research for both books.

Baptism before Jesus’ birth?

Alma 4:4 describes baptisms and Alma 7:10–15 presents the birth of Christ as future and calls for baptism. Mark 1:4 (KJV) describes John’s baptism of repentance. The Greek βάπτισμα (baptisma) denotes baptism and μετανοίας (metanoias) refers to repentance; see SBL Greek text and NET and NET notes. Mark does not explicitly say that no ritual washing ever occurred before John. The earlier Christian covenant baptism claimed by Alma is a historical and theological question, not a demonstrated logical contradiction merely because the Bible does not narrate it. No ancient original-language manuscript of Alma was verified for comparison; the English text is the evidence examined here.

Prophecy and interpretation

Isaiah 53:5 (KJV) concerns the suffering servant. The Hebrew מְחֹלָל (mecholal) permits wounded/pierced renderings; the NET comparison and notes and Hebrew and JPS allow different wording. Christians interpret the servant in relation to Jesus; Jewish readings may identify the servant differently. An ancient witness establishes the passage predates Jesus, not by itself the identity of the servant. A complete verse-by-verse comparison with Alma remains pending.

Why water in the sacrament?

The LDS Church explains its use of water through Doctrine and Covenants 27; its historical manual describes a gradual change. Whether that practice agrees with biblical teaching is a separate theological question. Further research: church governance, financial obligations, offerings, missionary rules and the distinction between correction and offense.
