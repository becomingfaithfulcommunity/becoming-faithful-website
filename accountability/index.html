Accountability & Spiritual Leadership

Love God. Love People.

A public statement of our commitments, not a claim that any human process is infallible.

Our foundation

Becoming Faithful seeks to love God and love people through the love described in 1 Corinthians 13:4–8 (KJV). Our goal is truth joined to patience, kindness, humility, and accountability. We are followers of Jesus Christ, not perfect judges of other people.

Each situation deserves careful consideration

We consider concerns one situation at a time rather than pretending one rigid procedure will fit every circumstance. Serious concerns should be reviewed by multiple people with differing perspectives. Reviewers should disclose conflicts of interest, examine the evidence, hear differing accounts where possible, and document their individual conclusions, including disagreement. No one person should conduct the entire investigation.

Privacy, transparency, and fairness

As founder, I voluntarily choose to share much of my own experience openly. I do not require others to surrender their privacy. People may ask for confidentiality or decline to speak publicly. We will accurately explain what information we were and were not able to examine, without treating privacy itself as evidence of dishonesty or guilt. Where possible, information can be reviewed confidentially by appropriate independent people. We must also protect sensitive information, especially where disclosure could cause harm.

Different conclusions are not erased

The review group records each supported conclusion and the reasoning behind it—even when one person disagrees with everyone else. In 1 Kings 22 and 2 Chronicles 18, Micaiah stood against approximately 400 prophets who agreed with one another. Their numerical agreement did not prove they were speaking truth. But being in the minority does not prove someone is right either: every claim must be examined. A leader considering the findings must be willing to ask whether they are acting like Micaiah or like Ahab, who rejected a warning he did not want to hear.

Prayer and final responsibility

After receiving the documented conclusions, the designated leader takes at least one full day to prayerfully reflect, seek the Holy Spirit’s guidance, weigh Scripture and evidence, and consider the effect on everyone involved. That leader then makes the final operational decision and the ministry proceeds accordingly. If that leader is personally involved or has a conflict of interest, an appropriate independent leader must decide instead. Urgent safety measures and reports to appropriate authorities must not be delayed while awaiting the day of prayer.

Obedience to authorities, freedom of faith, and the cost of following Christ

We respect the authorities and the laws and regulations established through the lawful governing process. When applicable law requires a report or cooperation with appropriate authorities, we will fulfill that responsibility. We will not use a private ministry investigation, prayer, or a claim of religious authority as a reason to conceal harm, obstruct a lawful investigation, or neglect required reporting. Scripture teaches respect for governing authorities (Romans 13:1–7; 1 Peter 2:13–17).

We also believe our highest allegiance belongs to Father God. If a government requirement directly conflicts with what we sincerely understand Scripture to command, we believe we must obey God rather than men (Acts 5:29). We will examine such questions carefully through Scripture, prayer, multiple perspectives, and appropriate legal counsel rather than presume every disagreement with a law is a conflict with God’s Word.

This protection is found in the First Amendment to the United States Constitution (Bill of Rights, Amendment I), specifically its Free Exercise Clause: “Congress shall make no law respecting an establishment of religion, or prohibiting the free exercise thereof.” The amendment also protects speech, peaceful assembly, and petition. The Congressional Constitution Annotated explains the Religion Clauses and their application to federal and state governments. These protections do not create an unconditional exemption from every law. The legal protections and obligations in a particular situation depend on the facts and applicable federal, state, and local law. We will seek to exercise our freedoms responsibly and remain truthful about possible legal consequences.

Jesus taught that following Him has a cost and urged His disciples to count that cost (Luke 14:27–33; Matthew 16:24–26). Every decision carries consequences, whether good, difficult, expected, or unforeseen. We will not promise that obedience to Christ always brings comfort, public approval, or safety in this earthly life. When faithfulness brings hardship, we seek to face it without threatening others, denying their rights, or ignoring our own accountability. Our trust remains in God even when we do not see the outcome here on earth.

God can speak; His message must be tested

We do not place human limits on what Father God or the Holy Spirit may do. God guides through Scripture, wisdom, prayer, counsel, and—as He wills—may communicate directly through people. Yet no leader’s claim to speak for God makes that person incapable of error. Scripture instructs believers to test spiritual claims (1 John 4:1), weigh prophetic speech (1 Corinthians 14:29), and prove all things (1 Thessalonians 5:21). Questioning a human interpretation is not automatically questioning God.

Decisions, correction, and judgment

A final decision provides direction; it is not a claim of perfection. Significant new evidence or demonstrated misconduct may warrant a fresh review. We seek to correct wrongs, protect people, and tell the truth in love. We ultimately entrust what remains beyond human knowledge and control to God, remembering that every one of us must give account to Him (Romans 14:12). Trusting God does not excuse inaction when we can prevent harm.

Scripture references (King James Version)

These passages were discussed as the biblical basis and cautions for these commitments. The links open the English King James Version (KJV). The brief summaries below are explanations, not quotations or official Spanish Bible translations.

  • 1 Corinthians 13:4–8 — Love is patient, kind, humble, and rejoices in truth.
  • Luke 12:2 — What is concealed will ultimately be revealed; this is not permission to expose private information indiscriminately.
  • Proverbs 18:17 — Hear and examine more than the first account.
  • 1 Timothy 5:19 — Accusations against elders require careful corroboration; this does not prohibit protecting people or reporting harm.
  • Matthew 18:15–17 — Seek private correction where appropriate, then wider involvement if needed.
  • Ecclesiastes 12:14 — God ultimately judges hidden and visible deeds.
  • Proverbs 11:14 — There is safety in a multitude of counselors.
  • Proverbs 15:22 — Good counsel strengthens plans and decisions.
  • Romans 14:12 — Each person gives account to God.
  • Proverbs 3:5–6 — Trust the Lord, not solely our own understanding.
  • James 1:5 — Ask God for wisdom.
  • Hebrews 13:17 — Spiritual leaders bear responsibility and must give account.
  • 1 John 4:1 — Test claims of spiritual guidance.
  • 1 Thessalonians 5:21 — Test everything and hold on to what is good.
  • 1 Corinthians 14:29 — Even prophetic speech is to be weighed by others.
  • John 16:13 — The Spirit of truth guides believers into truth.
  • Acts 13:2 — Scripture describes the Holy Spirit directing believers.
  • 1 Corinthians 12:11 — The Spirit distributes gifts as He wills.
  • 1 Kings 22 — Micaiah stands against the roughly 400 prophets of Ahab; majority agreement alone does not establish truth.
  • 2 Chronicles 18 — The parallel account of Micaiah, Ahab, and Jehoshaphat.
  • John 13:34–35 — Jesus commands His followers to love one another.
  • John 15:12–13 — Jesus commands love modeled after His own.
  • 1 John 4:7–11 — God’s love is the source and example of believers’ love.
  • Ephesians 4:15 — Speak truth in love.
  • Romans 13:1–7 — Respect for governing authorities and their responsibilities.
  • 1 Peter 2:13–17 — Submission to lawful human institutions and honoring others.
  • Acts 5:29 — When earthly authority contradicts obedience to God, the apostles affirm obedience to God.
  • Luke 14:27–33 — Jesus teaches disciples to count the cost of following Him.
  • Matthew 16:24–26 — Taking up the cross and following Jesus can require sacrifice.
