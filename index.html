What is Becoming Faithful?

Becoming Faithful is a growing ministry built on the foundation of Jesus Christ, centered on the definition of love found in 1 Corinthians 13, as we seek to learn how to love God and love people well.

Beginning in August 2025, I started working as a Hospitality Host at Chick Fil A in Tucson, Arizona. Over the following year, working around 30 or more hours each week, I had the opportunity to meet and have conversations with people from many different walks of life. Many of those conversations started with a simple question: "How is your day going?" Whether someone answered their day was good or bad, I would often ask what had made their day that way.

When they asked about my day, I tried to be as honest and transparent as I could be, not only with them but with myself as well. Some days that meant sharing something I was excited about or a success in my life, while other days it meant being open about a struggle, mistake, or failure and allowing my emotions to show instead of trying to hide how I was really feeling. Over time, I began noticing that people who initially seemed guarded would often become more open as I was willing to be open with them. They were more willing to extend grace, seek to understand where I was coming from, and work through my mistakes and misunderstanding together. Through those experiences and connections, the heart behind Becoming Faithful was starting to form.

As I began connecting with people online through social media, starting with Instagram and, more importantly, its connected platform Threads, those same lessons about honesty, transparency and importance of human connection face to face began following me there too. As those connections grew, I started to value the power of prayer and began asking God for guidance, as well as asking people from all over the world to pray for me. It opened my eyes to something I knew was technically there but had never paid attention to: time zones. It made me realize that the world is so much bigger than I thought, but at the same time, so small when you are intentional about looking for and connecting with people. Those experiences started changing the way I prayed, the way I saw God the Father, Jesus Christ the Son, and the Holy Spirit, the way I saw people and began learning the importance of loving them the way God does, and the way I began learning the importance of connection and community. On August 1,2026, courage began looking different to me.

"Have not I commanded thee? Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid, neither be thou dismayed: for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest." -Joshua 1:9 King James Version"

I am using the King James Version here because it is the translation I was reading and reflecting on during this part of the Journey. I am not using it to suggest that other Bible translations are wrong, but to make clear the wording and perspective that was shaping my understanding at the time.

For me, that meant being willing to step away from the comfort and stability I had found at Chick Fil A, including the security of knowing I could pay my bills,and begin stepping into something I did not fully know how to build yet. I was grateful to leave with the owner's blessings and prayers with no hard feelings. This was not about running away from Chick fil A, I still plan on staying in contact with the owner, to help him where I can, to keep learning from the different perspectives he has helped me see, and to offer perspectives that may help him as well. For me this was about me stepping out in faith to be obedient to what God was calling me to pursue.

Becoming Faithful becaming a starting point for something I hope continues far beyound me- a growing community of people learning to love God, love others, encourage one another, grow in courage together, obidence to God, and faithfully take part in building His Kingdom for as long as he allows it to grow, one person at a time. And that one person is not just part of the mission- that person is vital to it. Your personal story mstters. Your personal growth matters. Your personal questions matter. The perspective you bring matters too-not because any one of us sees everything perfectly, but because each person sees life through experiences, questions, lessons, and circumstances that others may not share. By listening to understand where someone is coming from - not simply waiting for our turn to respond - asking questions, and remaining willing to learn and receive correction rooted in love, we can help one another grow. That kind of love is the standard described in 1 Corinthians 13, King James Version.

Built From the Beginning

This is the first website I have ever built. I started with no experience building a website from scratch, and I continue choosing to learn it one step at a time. After a few weeks of intentional work—many days spending 10 to 17 hours learning, building, testing, making mistakes, fixing them, and trying again—this is what that process has become.

But the story behind this website is also connected to a much bigger transition in my life.

August 1, 2026

On August 1, 2026, I reached out to the owner of the Chick-fil-A where I worked and asked about taking a three-month leave of absence so I could pursue Becoming Faithful. He told me that I had his support and prayers behind me. He could not guarantee that a job would be available if I decided to return after those three months, but he was open to having that conversation when the time came.

Around the same time, I had asked management for August 6 and 7 off so I could attend the Global Leadership Summit, I had not received an answer.

August 4, 2026

On Tuesday, August 4, I went to work like I normally did. At one point, I was cleaning an entrance door that most people did not use. There was a man outside experiencing homelessness. I felt God put it on my heart to ask him if he wanted something to eat.

He said yes.

I asked him what he wanted, and he told me, “Whatever you want to give me.” I told him, “Nah man, I can get you what you want.”

After he told me what he wanted, I asked if he would like to come inside and get out of the heat while I got his food. He asked if I was sure. I told him, “Absolutely.”

And I asked his name.

His name is Brian.

That matters to me. Brian is not simply “a homeless man.” He is a person with a name, a story, and value.

Brian came inside, sat down, ate the meal I had purchased for him, and eventually left.

Afterward, management told me that I was not allowed to invite people experiencing homelessness into the restaurant to give them food. I explained that I had paid for Brian's meal myself and that I did not understand the issue. I also told them that if caring for someone in that situation was something I could not do, I was not sure I could continue being part of the company.

I was told to wait until HR arrived.

When HR came in, they asked me what happened. I told them exactly what had happened. When I explained that I had invited Brian inside, I was handed a write-up stating that I had violated a safety policy.

I was also told that they had heard I wanted to leave and that, because of labor costs, I should clock out immediately and not return for the remainder of my scheduled shifts that week.

I said goodbye.

At 10:31 a.m. on August 4, 2026, my time working at Chick-fil-A came to an end.

Why I Made a Stand

The situation with Brian was not the only thing weighing on me. There had also been other smaller moments when I felt I was being asked by management to compromise standards I had set for myself.

At some point, I realized that if I was unwilling to make a stand for what I believed while my responsibilities were still relatively limited, it would probably become harder as life went on.

Part of that realization came from listening to customers and hearing their stories. I had conversations with people about work, bills, family, responsibilities, decisions they had made, and circumstances they never expected to stay in. Those conversations stayed with me.

I was also investing in mentorship at the time. I had invested $7,000 to learn from people who had built businesses and careers earning more than $1 million. One message I heard repeatedly was that I was allowing myself to remain stuck within the confines of my Chick-fil-A job instead of fully pursuing what I believed I was being called to build.

I did not take that as meaning there was something wrong with working at Chick-fil-A or with having a regular job. The question for me was whether I was using the security and structure of that job as a reason to keep delaying decisions I already knew I needed to make.

Between those conversations, the stories customers had shared with me, the smaller compromises I felt I was making, and eventually what happened with Brian, I could see a pattern forming. I knew that if I kept telling myself, “Later,” it could become much harder to change direction once I had more responsibilities and more people depending on me.

I began to understand how easily more responsibilities, more financial obligations, and more people depending on me could make it easier to keep telling myself, “Not yet.” I could see how little compromises could slowly become normal, and how much harder it could become to walk away later from something I knew I should have addressed earlier.

I did not want to enter that cycle.

So when the situation with Brian happened, it became more than one disagreement about one meal. For me, it forced a larger question: if I believed something was right, was I actually willing to accept a cost for standing by it?

Leaving my job did cost me. It hurt then, and even as I write this on August 14, 2026, there are still real consequences from that decision.

But the hurt I feel now is nothing compared with what I believe I would have felt if I had stayed and, little by little, compromised the standards I had chosen to hold myself to.

I would rather deal honestly with the cost of a decision I believed I needed to make than look back years later and realize I had slowly become willing to surrender my convictions simply because walking away had become harder.

August 6–7, 2026

The unexpected ending of my time at Chick-fil-A opened the door for me to attend the Global Leadership Summit on August 6 and 7—the same event I had previously asked to have those days off to attend.

While sitting there and listening to leaders speak, something became clear to me:

I needed to go all in on Becoming Faithful.

Building Strong Foundations

So this website is more than a collection of HTML and CSS files. It is part of that decision.

I am learning as I build it. I am making mistakes, asking questions, fixing things, starting over when necessary, and trying to build strong foundations instead of rushing toward something that simply looks finished.

That process reflects what I hope Becoming Faithful itself will always be: a willingness to learn openly, live transparently, love God, love people, accept correction, and continue moving forward one step at a time.

How Becoming Faithful Defines Love

At the heart of Becoming Faithful is something simple: Love God and Love people. But what does that love actually look like? For that, I want to point back to 1 Corinthians 13 in the King James Version. The KJV usesthe word "charity" in this passage when describing this kinda of love.

Charity suffereth long, and is kind; charity envieth not; charity vaunteth not itself, is not puffed up, doth not behave itself unseemly, seeketh not her own, is not easily provoked, thinketh no evil; rejoiceth not in iniquity, but rejoiceth in the truth; beareth all things, believeth all things, hopeth all things, endureth all things. Charity never faileth." -1 Corinthians 13: 4-8, King James Version

So Scripture shows us both what love is and what love is not. Love is patient and kind. It rejoices in truth, bears, believes, hopes, and endures. Love is not envious, prideful, self seeking, easily provoked, or rejoicing in wrongdoing.

Scripture also shows why love is so vital. Paul writes that even if someone could speak with extraordinary gifts but did not have charity, he would become "as sounding brass, or a thinking cymbal." Even with great knowledge and faith, without charity, "I am nothing." And even after giving everything away, without cbarity, "it profiteth me nothing." -1 Corinthians 13:1-3, King James Version

That is why love is crucial and cannot simply be another part of Becoming Faithful. It has to be at the core of how we speak, listen, correct, encourage, serve, disagree, learn, and grow together. We will not do this perfectly, but the goal is to keep trying, keep learning, keep growing, and keep moving forward - becoming a little more faithful today than we were yesterday.

" And now abideth faith, hope, charity, these three; but the greatest of these is charity." - 1 Corinthians 13:13, King James Version

How We Grow

Becoming Faithful focuses on five areas of Growth: Love, Hope, Faith, Wisdom, and Everyday Life.

Love

Learning to love God and love people more faithfully in the way we speak, listen, serve, forgive, encourage, and live each day.

Hope

Learning to holdonto hope in God, especially when life feels uncertain, difficult, or different from what we expected

Faith

Learning to trust God more deeply, follow Him faithfully, and keep moving forward even when we do not have every answer or know exactly what comes next.

Wisdom

Learning to seek God's wisdom, ask better questions, listen carefully, learn from others, accept correction, and make thoughtful choices in everyday life

Everyday Life

Learning to live out what we believe in ordinary life through our choices, relationships, work, finances, habits, responsibilities, and the way we treat people each day.

What This Looks Like in Every day Life.

Becoming Faithful is not only about what we believe, but how those beliefs shape the way we live each day.

Growth can happen in every opportunity and situation, whether it be in the way we speak to people, handle disagreements, make decisions, use our time and money, respond to mistakes, or keep moving forward when life is difficult. But growth can also move us in the wrong direction. That is why perspective and intentionality matter. We want to intentionally grow in the kind of love the Bible describes in 1 Corinthians 13—becoming more patient, kind, truthful, humble, enduring, and faithful in the way we live.

Choosing the Direction We Grow

Every experience can shape us. Difficulties can make us more compassionate or more bitter. Disagreements can teach us to listen and understand, or cause us to become more defensive. Mistakes can teach us humility and wisdom, or become something we stubbornly refuse to learn from.

When we respond through love, it can help cast out fear, as 1 John 4:18 says, “perfect love casteth out fear.” But when we respond through bitterness, defensiveness, or stubbornness, fear can grow, and that fear can lead us back into the same responses, creating a cycle that becomes difficult to break.

That is why we want to intentionally grow toward the love described in 1 Corinthians 13. Love that is patient, kind, truthful, humble, and enduring does not require us to hide our mistakes, questions, or struggles. Instead, it can give us the foundation to live transparently, receive correction, learn from what happens, and continue moving forward.

Learning Through Everyday Life

This means everyday life becomes an opportunity to learn. Our relationships with family, friends, coworkers, people in our communities, strangers, people we disagree with, and those we may only interact with briefly can all reveal opportunities for growth. Even brief interactions can have a lasting impact. As people, we know that first impressions can stay with us for a long time. A single negative experience can sometimes make someone hesitant to reach out, trust, or try again. A simple example is going to a restaurant one time, having a bad experience, and deciding that you never want to go back—even though that one experience may not represent every person or every future experience there. That is another reason why how we treat people matters. We may not always know what someone has experienced before meeting us, what impression our interaction may leave with them, or whether we will ever have another opportunity to interact with them. Our work, finances, decisions, successes, failures, questions, relationships, and even ordinary moments can all reveal areas where we are growing and areas where we still need to grow.

Growth is a Continuing Process

None of this means we will respond perfectly in every situation. We will make mistakes, misunderstand people, react poorly at times, and discover areas where we still need to grow. Even the wisdom and knowledge we gain can become an opportunity for pride if we begin believing that we already know enough, have all the answers, or no longer need to listen and learn from others. Pride can cause us to react poorly, resist correction, and become less willing to recognize when we may be wrong.

Remaining open to learning also does not mean that every idea or interpretation must be accepted as true. When something appears to go against the Bible, we do not want to simply disregard the person or their thought. Instead, we want to listen and understand where they believe their idea comes from in Scripture, how they came to understand or interpret it that way, and allow room for honest and respectful discussion about what the passage means. We should also be willing to explain where our own understanding comes from and remain humble enough to examine whether we may have misunderstood something ourselves.

Through those conversations, our ultimate standard is not our pride, personal opinion, tradition, or desire to be right. We continually return to the Word of God, examining Scripture in its context and seeking to understand what it actually teaches.

The goal is not perfection. The goal is to remain humble and willing to learn, accept correction, admit when we are wrong, make things right when possible, and intentionally keep moving forward—trying each day to become a little more faithful than the day before.

At the foundation of all of this is love—not simply love as we personally define it, but the love described in 1 Corinthians 13. Biblical love should shape how we listen, how we speak, how we disagree, how we receive correction, how we seek truth, and how we treat one another through the process. As we continually return to the Word of God, we also continually return to the heart of it: learning to love God and love people more faithfully.

Our Mission

The mission of Becoming Faithful is to Love God and Love People by following Jesus and encouraging one another to grow in love, hope, faith, wisdom, and everyday life.

Becoming Faithful is a place to learn openly, live transparently, ask honest questions, pursue accountability, and keep growing together. We do not claim to have everything figured out. We want to share the lessons, mistakes, questions, and growth along the way, remain willing to listen and be corrected, and keep moving forward one step at a time.

This space is open to everyone—whether you are simply curious, a believer, or unsure of what you believe. You are welcome to learn alongside us, ask questions, share your perspective, and see what following Jesus can look like in everyday life.

Above everything else, we want Becoming Faithful to remain centered on loving God and loving people. Becoming Faithful should never become something we protect for its own sake. If protecting the name ever requires us to stop loving people, ignore truth, resist correction, or lose sight of God, then we have lost the very reason Becoming Faithful exists.

Our Commitments

Becoming Faithful is not built on the idea that we will always get everything right. We are committed to remaining humble, learning openly, living transparently, asking honest questions, listening to understand, and being willing to receive correction.

We want accountability to come not only from people who agree with us, but also from people who disagree with us or criticize us. Truth does not become less important because of who speaks it. We want to listen, examine what is being said, return to Scripture, and change when correction is needed.

When we are wrong, we want to be willing to say, "We were wrong," make things right when possible, learn from it, and keep moving forward. Protecting the image, name, influence, following, or future of Becoming Faithful should never become more important than loving God, loving people, and telling the truth in love.

These commitments are not a claim that we will live them out perfectly. They are standards we want to continually return to and invite others to hold us accountable to.

How You Can Participate

Becoming Faithful starts with one person. For me, that means it starts with me choosing each day to Love God and Love People more faithfully. From there, the hope is that one person impacts another person, who impacts another, and it continues one person at a time.

You do not have to give money to be part of Becoming Faithful. More than financial support, I want to see people become part of a community that is genuinely trying to Love God and Love People in everyday life. If one person chooses to love, listen, encourage, forgive, serve, learn, or help someone—and that impacts another person who does the same—that matters.

Another way you can help is simply by sharing and spreading the word. You can share Becoming Faithful with someone personally or share its posts, videos, stories, and resources across social media platforms. The purpose is not simply to grow numbers or gain followers. The hope is that sharing helps reach another person who may need encouragement, have a story to share, want to ask questions, or want to grow alongside the community.

Supporting the Work

Becoming Faithful will continue whether or not financial support comes in. Support can create more time and opportunity to listen, learn, create, connect with people, and reach more people one person at a time.

For anyone who chooses to support financially, the standard invitation is US$1 a month. Giving more is always voluntary and never gives anyone more influence, priority, or control over Becoming Faithful.

Financial support is only one way to participate. Sharing Becoming Faithful, encouraging another person, contributing your skills, asking questions, and choosing to Love God and Love People more faithfully all matter too.
